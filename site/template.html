Alpha-RTL adapts the TTT-Discover recipe to hardware. For a single RTL module, it samples rewrites from an LLM, checks each one with simulation, synthesis and static timing, and updates the model's weights on that feedback, so the next round of rewrites starts from what the EDA tools taught it. We point it at hand-written floating-point units from the open-source XuanTie C910 RISC-V core.

Method

The training loop

A design-state archive holds every verified candidate. Each step, Alpha-RTL picks parents from it, asks the policy for rewrites, scores the rewrites with real EDA tools, and takes a policy-gradient step before sampling again.

  1. SelectPUCT picks parent designs from the archive, trading reward against how often a design was expanded.
  2. RewriteQwen3-8B reads the parent RTL and its tool feedback, then proposes a new module.
  3. VerifyIcarus Verilog compiles it and checks it against the C910 RTL on random and edge-case vectors.
  4. MeasureYosys maps it to standard cells; OpenSTA reports the critical path.
  5. LearnAn entropic policy-gradient update with a KL budget trains on the step's 32 rewards.

Verified rewrites go back into the archive, so later steps refine earlier wins.

reward = 0.1·[compiles] + 1.0·[matches reference] + 10 · ADPref / ADP

Verification

How we check a design before reporting it

Simulation during training samples a few hundred vectors. Every design on this page passed three stronger checks after training, run on the exact RTL we publish.

Formal equivalenceYosys builds a miter of the design and the C910 RTL; a SAT proof shows the outputs never differ (the FADD ff1 outputs are don't-care when the operands are equal, as in the spec).
Re-synthesisArea and delay on this page come from a fresh Yosys + OpenSTA run, not from training logs.
